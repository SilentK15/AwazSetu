1. PROBLEM STATEMENT & MOTIVATION
========================================================================================
The Core Problem with Existing Municipal Grievance Portals (CPGRAMS, BMC, Swachhata):
- Ticket Flooding: When a water main breaks or a major pothole appears, 50 citizens report 
  the same issue. Traditional portals create 50 separate tickets, clogging the queue and 
  dispatching multiple inspection teams to the same spot.
- Incorrect Routing: Citizens often select the wrong department dropdown (e.g. selecting 
  "Sanitation" for a leaking sewer pipe instead of "Water Supply & Sewerage"). Tickets bounce 
  between departments for weeks.
- Lack of Urgency Differentiation: First-Come-First-Serve queues treat an open manhole on 
  a school road with the same priority as a non-functioning streetlight in an empty park.
- Black-Box AI: Officials do not trust automated routing unless the system explains *why* 
  a ticket was assigned or prioritized.

AwazSetu's Solution:
- Solves all 4 problems using lightweight, low-latency, on-device AI algorithms without 
  expensive cloud API dependencies or manual human sorting.

3. DETAILED AI & MATHEMATICAL MODELS (EXACT FORMULAS)

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 MULTILINGUAL NORMALIZATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Goal: Allow citizens to write in their native language (Hindi, Marathi, or English).
- Implementation: 
  - Detects Devanagari Unicode script range (\u0900-\u097F).
  - Uses localized translation dictionaries and fallback normalization pipelines to translate 
    regional terms (e.g., 'गंदगी', 'कचरा', 'खड्डा', 'पाणी गळती', 'विजेचा खांब') into normalized English.
- Why?: Standardizes inputs so the SentenceTransformer model can perform cross-lingual semantic matching accurately.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 SENTENCE-TRANSFORMERS & SEMANTIC EMBEDDINGS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Model: `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (SentenceTransformers framework).
- Architecture: 6-layer MiniLM transformer with 22.7 million parameters.
- Embedding Dimension: 384 dimensions.
- Inference Speed: ~15 milliseconds on standard CPU (zero GPU needed).
- Department Classification:
  1. Solid Waste Management (Garbage, trash overflow, dead animals, illegal dumping)
  2. Roads & Traffic Infrastructure (Potholes, broken asphalt, traffic signal dead, footpaths)
  3. Water Supply & Sewerage (Pipeline burst, contaminated drinking water, open sewer)
  4. Public Health & Sanitation (Mosquito breeding, stagnant water, dengue/malaria risk)
  5. Electricity & Street Lighting (Streetlight blackouts, exposed live wire, sparking box)

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 400-METRE SPATIO-TEMPORAL DUPLICATE DETECTION (HAVERSINE + COSINE SIMILARITY)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When a complaint is submitted at (lat1, lon1) with embedding vector E1:
The system queries all active complaints from SQLite and applies a two-tier filter:

Step A: Spatial Proximity via Haversine Formula:
   a = sin²(Δlat / 2) + cos(lat1) * cos(lat2) * sin²(Δlon / 2)
   c = 2 * atan2(√a, √(1 - a))
   Distance d = R * c   (where R = 6,371,000 meters)

   Condition: d <= 400 meters. (If distance > 400m, skip immediately).

Step B: Semantic Similarity via Cosine Distance:
   Cosine Similarity(E1, E2) = (E1 • E2) / (||E1|| * ||E2||)

   Condition: Cosine Similarity >= 0.80 (80% semantic match).

Outcome:
- If BOTH conditions match: The new complaint is classified as a DUPLICATE.
- Action: It is linked as a child to the Parent Ticket (`parent_id = P001`), and adds +1 community confirmation upvote to the parent, bumping its urgency score by +8 points without clogging the authority queue.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4 DYNAMIC PRIORITY & URGENCY SCORING FORMULA (0 - 100 SCALE)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Priority is calculated using a multi-factor deterministic equation:

Severity Score = Base_Score 
                 + Sentiment_Penalty 
                 + Hazard_Keyword_Bonus 
                 + Photo_Evidence_Bonus 
                 + Duplicate_Upvote_Bonus

Parameters:
1. Base Score (by Department Risk):
   - Public Health / Electricity: 38 points
   - Water & Sewerage / Roads: 34 points
   - Solid Waste: 30 points

2. Sentiment Penalty (VADER Polarity Compound Score: -1.0 to +1.0):
   - Extreme Negative (-1.0 to -0.6): +15 points (high citizen distress)
   - Moderate Negative (-0.6 to -0.2): +8 points
   - Neutral / Mild (-0.2 to +1.0): 0 points

3. Hazard Keyword Triggers:
   - Level 1 Hazard ("accident", "sparking", "death", "hospital", "children", "fire", "emergency"): +22 points
   - Level 2 Hazard ("overflow", "dengue", "dark", "school", "elderly", "blocked"): +12 points

4. Photo Evidence Flag:
   - Photo Attached: +10 points (verified physical evidence)

5. Community Confirmations (Upvotes / Merged Duplicates):
   - +8 points per confirmation (capped at +24 points)

Priority Categorization:
- 80 to 100 points: CRITICAL (Red SLA: <12 Hours)
- 60 to 79 points:  HIGH     (Orange SLA: <24 Hours)
- 40 to 59 points:  MEDIUM   (Yellow SLA: <48 Hours)
- 0 to 39 points:   LOW      (Green SLA: <72 Hours)

6. DATABASE SCHEMA & SECURITY
========================================================================================
The platform uses two lightweight, zero-maintenance SQLite databases:

1. `civic_grievance.db` (Table: `grievances`)
   - `id` (TEXT, PK): e.g., 'CG-984210'
   - `original_text` (TEXT): Raw input in native language
   - `text_en` (TEXT): Normalized English text
   - `detected_lang` (TEXT): 'en', 'hi', or 'mr'
   - `department` (TEXT): Assigned department
   - `ward` (TEXT): Municipal ward name
   - `lat`, `lon` (REAL): GPS Coordinates
   - `priority` (TEXT): 'Critical', 'High', 'Medium', 'Low'
   - `severity_score` (INTEGER): 0 to 100
   - `status` (TEXT): 'Pending', 'In Progress', 'Resolved'
   - `upvotes` (INTEGER): Community confirmation count
   - `is_duplicate` (INTEGER): 0 or 1
   - `parent_id` (TEXT): Reference to parent ticket
   - `embedding` (TEXT): Serialized 384-dimensional JSON vector
   - `xai_department` (TEXT): Explainability text for routing
   - `xai_priority` (TEXT): Explainability text for scoring
   - `image_flag` (INTEGER): 0 or 1
   - `citizen_phone` (TEXT): Reporter phone number
   - `created_at` (TEXT): ISO timestamp

2. `civic_users.db` (Table: `users`)
   - `id` (TEXT, PK), `role` (TEXT), `username` (TEXT), `password_hash` (TEXT), `salt` (TEXT), `full_name` (TEXT)
   - Passwords hashed using SHA-256 with unique 16-character cryptographic hex salts.

7. TOP 20 QUESTIONS JUDGES WILL ASK & WINNING ANSWERS
========================================================================================

Q1: "How does your duplicate detection work?"
A1: "We use a two-tier spatial-semantic algorithm. First, we compute the Haversine distance 
between the new complaint and all existing complaints in the database. If distance is within 
400 meters, we compute Cosine Similarity between their 384-dimensional SentenceTransformer 
embeddings. If similarity exceeds 0.80, it is flagged as a duplicate, linked to the parent ticket, 
and increments an upvote to elevate priority without creating redundant work orders."

Q2: "Why 400 meters? What if an issue spans a larger area?"
A2: "400 meters corresponds to the standard walkable radius (~5-minute walk) for localized civic 
disturbances like garbage piles, potholes, or broken streetlights. For linear issues like 
pipeline bursts, multiple clusters form along the line, which our Mapbox heatmap immediately 
visualizes as a continuous thermal corridor."

Q3: "What NLP model do you use for classification?"
A3: "We use SentenceTransformers with the `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` architecture. It maps complaints 
into a 384-dimensional dense semantic vector space. It runs in ~15ms on CPU, making it extremely 
cost-efficient and lightweight for government deployment without requiring costly GPUs."

Q4: "How do you handle Indian languages like Hindi and Marathi?"
A4: "We have an automatic language detection and normalization pipeline. Hindi and Marathi 
Devanagari inputs are detected via Unicode ranges and normalized into standardized English 
representations before embedding, ensuring semantic consistency across diverse languages."

Q5: "How is priority scored? Is it just random or heuristic?"
A5: "It uses a deterministic 5-factor mathematical formula: Base Department Risk (30-38 pts) + 
VADER Sentiment Distress (+0-15 pts) + Hazard Keywords (+12-22 pts) + Photo Evidence (+10 pts) + 
Community Upvotes (+8 pts/upvote). The resulting 0–100 score maps into Critical, High, Medium, 
and Low tiers with strict SLAs."

Q6: "What is Explainable AI (XAI) in your portal and why is it important?"
A6: "In government systems, officials cannot trust 'black-box' predictions. Our XAI engine 
generates an exact breakdown of every point awarded (e.g. +15 for negative sentiment, +22 for 
hazard keywords like 'sparking'). This creates total auditability for authorities and builds trust with citizens."

Q7: "What happens when a citizen selects the wrong department?"
A7: "The AI overrides the user's category hint based on semantic vector matching against 
department archetypes. In the XAI log, it explicitly notes: 'Citizen selected Sanitation, 
but AI classified as Roads & Traffic Infrastructure due to keyword pothole'."

Q8: "How does the Mapbox Heatmap help municipal authorities?"
A8: "The Heatmap uses kernel density estimation weighted by each ticket's severity score and upvotes. 
Instead of looking at raw spreadsheets, authorities immediately see bright red hotspots where 
critical clusters exist, allowing proactive dispatch of repair crews."

Q9: "How do you obtain the citizen's location? What if GPS is denied?"
A9: "We use HTML5 browser geolocation via `streamlit_js_eval`, which requests real-time device 
satellite coordinates (latitude/longitude with accuracy in meters). If GPS permission is closed, 
the system seamlessly falls back to administrative zone detection."

Q10: "Is the data persistent or does it reset on server restart?"
A10: "All complaints, embeddings, status changes, upvotes, and user accounts are permanently stored 
in ACID-compliant SQLite databases (`civic_grievance.db` and `civic_users.db`). Status updates by 
officials persist instantly across user sessions."

Q11: "What prevents someone from spamming upvotes or fake complaints?"
A11: "1) Users must be authenticated with mobile credentials. 2) Duplicate submissions within 
400m are automatically merged as upvotes rather than new tickets. 3) Upvote score boosts are 
capped at +24 points to prevent artificial inflation."

Q12: "How scalable is this architecture?"
A12: "SentenceTransformers embeddings are computed once on submission (15ms). SQLite easily handles 
100,000+ records with indexed spatial queries. For national scale (millions of records), SQLite 
can be swapped to PostgreSQL with PostGIS and pgvector in under 2 hours without altering any AI logic."

Q13: "What role-based access control (RBAC) is implemented?"
A13: "We maintain strict segregation: Citizens only see the submission form and their own tracked 
tickets; Authorities have access to the Operations Dashboard, Mapbox Heatmap, and 1-Click Status 
Redressal Controls. Passwords use SHA-256 with unique 16-character cryptographic salts."

Q14: "Why did you build this in Streamlit instead of a heavy React/Node framework?"
A14: "Streamlit enables 100% native Python execution, eliminating REST API overhead, serialization 
delays, and multi-server desynchronization. The AI models, spatial math, database, and UI execute 
in a single unified runtime with zero external server dependencies."

Q15: "How does your system handle photos/images uploaded by citizens?"
A15: "Uploaded photos serve two purposes: 1) Verified physical evidence adds +10 points to the 
urgency score. 2) The photo is stored and displayed in both the Citizen Tracking card and 
Authority Queue for visual inspection before dispatch."

Q16: "What is your SLA (Service Level Agreement) strategy?"
A16: "Critical (80-100 score) tickets trigger a 12-hour SLA with prominent visual badges. 
High (60-79) has a 24-hour SLA. The admin queue automatically sorts unaddressed high-priority 
tickets to the top of the queue."

Q17: "Can a citizen track merged complaints?"
A17: "Yes! When a citizen's complaint is merged into a parent ticket, they receive a confirmation 
banner: 'Merged into Ticket #CG-XXXX (92.4% match)'. They can track the parent ticket's live resolution."

Q18: "What happens when an admin marks a ticket as Resolved?"
A18: "The database status changes from 'Pending' or 'In Progress' to 'Resolved'. The ticket is 
removed from the active priority queue, its thermal weight on the heatmap diminishes, and the citizen 
sees a green 'Resolved' status badge in real time."

Q19: "How is AwazSetu different from existing apps like BMC 24x7 or Swachhata App?"
A19: "Existing apps are passive forms connected to manual sorting queues. AwazSetu is an active AI 
decision-support system with semantic auto-routing, dynamic multi-factor urgency scoring, 
explainable AI audit logs, and automatic 400m spatial-semantic deduplication."

Q20: "What is your roadmap for future enhancements?"
A20: "1) Computer Vision pipeline (YOLOv8) to auto-verify pothole depth and garbage volume from photos. 
2) WhatsApp Bot interface for complaint submission via voice notes. 3) Automated SMS notifications 
to citizens upon ticket resolution."
